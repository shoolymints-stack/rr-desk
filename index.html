Round Robin DeskPAPER

NCAA men's basketball. One 4-team round robin a day, only when 4 legs clear a 3.3% edge.

The method

  • Fair price. Pinnacle's line with the vig removed, or the middle of 3 or more books at the same number.
  • Edge. Fair win chance minus the win chance the offered price needs. A leg needs 3.3% or better (A+).
  • One leg per game. Moneyline, spread or total, whichever has the biggest edge. Prices from -120 to +300.
  • One ticket. 4 legs from 4 games, all at the same book, played as a round robin by 2s: 6 two-team parlays. Fewer than 4 legs at one book means no ticket that day.
  • Singles. Every qualifying leg is also tracked alone. That shows whether the edge is real much sooner than tickets can.

Reading the record

  • 1R is the stake. A ticket's R is its net result divided by the 6 units staked.
  • Expectancy is the average R per play. Above zero means the method makes money per play on average.
  • Hit rate vs break-even compares how often legs win with how often they must win at the prices taken.
  • CLV checks the fair price about 10 minutes before tip. Positive means the price taken beat the close, the best early sign of a real edge.

What to expect

  • Most days have no ticket. Four legs clearing 3.3% at one book is uncommon.
  • A round robin loses its full stake when 0 or 1 legs win, and loses a little when 2 win. The profit comes from 3 and 4 leg days.
  • Nothing here is a guarantee. Judge it on 100 or more singles and 30 or more tickets, on paper, before any money goes on it.
